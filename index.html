How touch typing actually works

It is not a talent. It is a map you stop looking at because your hands already know the way.

Eyes stay on the text

Every glance at the keys resets the learning. The board is for your fingers. The screen is for your attention.

Each finger owns a zone

You do not hunt with whichever finger is closest. You send the owner, then return to F and J.

Accuracy before speed

Speed is what happens after the motion is clean. Rushing early teaches the wrong pattern.

Muscle memory is repetition

A calm ten minutes a day beats a frustrated hour. The hands learn from what you repeat.

Home is the whole system

A S D F and J K L ; are not beginner toys. They are the docking station for every other key.

Mistakes are information

A miss tells you which reach is still fuzzy. TypeFlow tracks those keys and builds drills from them.

Questions people actually ask

What is touch typing?

Typing with the home-row positions so you do not need to look at the keys. Each finger covers a vertical slice of the board and returns home after a reach.

How long does it take to learn?

Most people feel oriented in a week of short daily sessions. Comfortable speed takes longer. Ten focused minutes beat a weekend binge.

Should I look at the keyboard?

During the first sitting-and-hands lessons, glance once to place F and J. After that, look at the screen. Peeking is a habit you would have to unlearn.

Why are F and J important?

They have small raised bars. Your index fingers rest there. From those two points the rest of the keyboard is a known distance.

What is a good typing speed?

Around 40 WPM is enough for most school and office work. 60–80 is comfortable. Accuracy at 95% or better matters more than a spike of speed.

Should I focus on speed or accuracy?

Accuracy. Speed is a side effect of a clean pattern. Fast mistakes teach your hands the wrong map.

Can beginners use this?

Yes. Lesson 0 starts with sitting, hand placement, and finding F and J. You never have to face the whole keyboard on day one.
